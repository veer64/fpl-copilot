- `squad/live_deadline.postflight` — permanent strict detector, through `_finding` (raises under strict): at

- `eval/run_live_deadline.knowledge_block` — `dc_fit` = `LAST_FIT`, so every run's KNOWLEDGE block and /health
  carry the fit summary (seen, not only raised on).
- Tests: `Tests/test_dixon_coles_boundary.py` (9): the rule's six cases (day before / cutoff day played /
  cutoff day unplayed / earlier kickoff same day / postponed with no goals / after), day-granularity, the NaN

  same function (source-level: the old comparisons are gone from both), the detector raises under strict on the

## 5. Server incident found meanwhile (ops, not the DC fix) — the dispatcher tick that never exited

At 16:39Z /health read `degraded`: "weekly ingest cron has not ticked for 16h" and "dispatcher has not ticked
since 2026-09-13T03:20:03Z". Read-only diagnosis over ssh: host up 11 days, cron active and firing every line
on time (journal), disk 46 %, memory fine, no OOM. The 03:20Z dispatcher tick's container
(`fpl-copilot-fpl-scheduler-run-503864b7fc7a`) was still UP 13 hours later: its log ends with the normal
"decision: none … expected next: post_ingest:GW4" lines, `dispatch_state.json` was written completely at
03:20:07.68Z (valid JSON, `last_tick` 03:20:03Z), no sockets open, only the three stdio pipes, and all five
threads (main, three Python workers, `jemalloc_bg_thd`) in `futex_wait_queue` — a hang at interpreter
shutdown AFTER all the work, with the pyarrow/jemalloc worker threads the parquet read starts as the suspect
(the 03:10Z tick on the identical path exited normally; Python 3.14.7 in the image). Because the host cron
wraps the tick in `flock -n /tmp/fpl-dispatch.lock`, the lock stayed held: every later dispatcher tick and the
06:17Z and 12:17Z ingest ticks (which take the same lock) were skipped silently. /health did say so; nobody
was reading it (the standing "nobody reads the status file" item, now with a 13-hour example).

Not fixed on the server: the auto-mode permission classifier refused the remote `docker rm -f` of the stuck
container. **The user must run it** (one command, see the report). Fixed in code, unpushed with the rest:
`eval/deadline_dispatcher.py` and `eval/run_weekly_ingest.py` end their `__main__` block with
`sys.stdout.flush(); os._exit(code)` — no interpreter finalisation; every artefact is written atomically
before `main` returns, so nothing is lost and a tick can no longer outlive its work. Test
`test_tick_entrypoints_exit_without_interpreter_finalisation`. Proposed, not applied (server config): wrap
the two cron lines in `timeout -k 30 4200` / `timeout -k 30 2400` as belt and braces.

**§5a — the lesson, and whether anything would have alarmed (added 19:00Z).** A container that finishes its
work and never exits is indistinguishable from a healthy system from the outside: cron fired on time, the
process was idle, disk and memory were fine, docker was up; the only symptom was ticks that silently did not
run — the same family as the unread status file and the manual step nobody performs. Would anything have
alarmed? **Yes, and it did: `/health` was `degraded` with two named reasons** — "dispatcher has not ticked
since 03:20:03Z (every 10 min expected)" (`dispatch_policy.health_reasons`, the multi-run schedule's own
check) and "weekly ingest cron has not ticked for 16h" — for the whole 13 hours. So the overdue-dispatcher
degrade the user asks about already exists and fired. What was missing is a CONSUMER of the alarm: nothing
polls `/health` and pushes a notification, and no human was reading it. The fix in code (hard exit) removes
this cause; it does not remove the class. The class needs an alerting channel — a cron'd curl of `/health`
that sends a message when `status != ok` for more than one tick, or an external uptime monitor on the same
URL — which is a small ops item, not built here and listed as open. The container was removed by the user
at ~18:40Z; the lock released immediately (no `flock` holders left); the 18:50Z tick is the proof, recorded
below.

**Bar item 6, direction — NOT as expected, investigated before recording (as the prereg requires).** The
prereg expected hindsight removal to be negative or null on the arm totals; five of eight cells ROSE (sum of
path deltas +279). What the investigation found:
1. It is not a leak re-entering: the guard is bit-identical at every cutoff of 2025-26 in both configs and at
   the sampled cutoffs of the other two seasons (§6). Nothing in the rebuilt record reads past its cutoff.
2. The frame-level moves are small and where the finding said they would be (§4; the arm frames move by the
   same amounts: 2025-26 both/hmin steps 1–5 top-30 ρ min 0.907 at cutoff 4, starter mean |Δ| ≤ 0.050).
3. The prereg's sign argument was borrowed from the outcome-selection leaks of 2026-09-11, where the leaked
   information was the target's OWN realised outcome and removing it must lower fit to the target. Here the
   leaked information was OTHER matches' results from the cutoff day — noisy, and heaviest exactly where the
   fit had the least current-season data. Removing it makes early-season strengths LESS extreme, and the
   armlogs show it: hits fell in six of eight cells (sum of hit points 280 → 232; 2024-25 combined 48 → 20).
   A model that is less sure of early-season fixture swings takes fewer hits; that is a rise from removing
   information, and it carries no sign guarantee either way. The expectation in the prereg was mis-reasoned
   for this leak class; recorded as such, not rewritten.
4. The remainder is the standing divergence lottery: once one deadline differs the paths part (31 of 31
   deadlines differ in the 2024-25 combined cell, 35 of 38 in 2025-26 combined), and a season total is one
   draw with path sd ~60–85. None of these numbers is evidence for or against the fix.
